Passage & Introductory Stem Question (i) [Understanding] & Answer Question (ii) [Application] & Answer Question (iii) [Analysis] & Answer
1. Plant physiology is a captivating branch of biology that delves into the life functions of plants. It explores the intricacies of how plants function, from the cellular level to the complex interactions of organ systems. This field unveils the secrets behind the growth, development, and survival of plants. “Plant physiology is the study of the vital processes within a plant, which are foundational to its survival.” Define the term 'Plant Physiology'. [Understanding] Answer: Plant physiology is the branch of biology that studies the life functions, metabolic processes, and physical and chemical processes occurring in plants, from the cellular level to the organ system level. Scenario: A botanist observes a plant suddenly wilting despite ample watering. Which specific area of plant physiology would be most relevant to investigate the cause? [Application] Answer: The area of Water Relations/Absorption and Transport would be most relevant, as wilting indicates a failure in maintaining turgidity, which is directly linked to the balance of water absorption and loss (transpiration/transport). How does the study of plant physiology, particularly root absorption, contribute to sustainable agriculture? [Analysis] Answer: Understanding root absorption mechanisms (like active transport of minerals) allows scientists to develop fertilizers and irrigation techniques that maximize nutrient and water uptake efficiency, thereby minimizing resource wastage and promoting sustainable crop yields.
2. Water is a crucial raw material for photosynthesis, the process by which plants convert sunlight into energy. Without sufficient water, plants cannot produce the food they need to survive and thrive. “Water is indispensable for the primary metabolic activity of a plant.” State the role of water as a raw material in photosynthesis. [Understanding] Answer: Water is essential for the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis, where it undergoes photolysis (splitting) to release electrons and hydrogen ions, which are necessary for the formation of ATP and NADPH, and oxygen as a byproduct. Scenario: During a prolonged drought, a plant closes its stomata. Explain the immediate impact this action has on the rate of photosynthesis. [Application] Answer: Closing the stomata reduces the plant's water loss (transpiration) but simultaneously restricts the uptake of carbon dioxide (CO2\text{CO}_2). Since CO2\text{CO}_2 is another crucial raw material for photosynthesis, its limited availability will lead to a drastic decrease in the rate of photosynthesis. Why is the concept of 'turgidity' considered a vital component of mechanical support in soft plant tissues? [Analysis] Answer: Turgidity is the state where the cell contents press against the cell wall due to water absorption. This internal pressure (turgor pressure) provides rigidity and firmness, enabling soft tissues like leaves and young stems to remain distended and stand upright, acting as a hydrostatic skeleton.
3. Transpiration, the release of water vapor from leaves, is essential for cooling plants and creating a suction force that draws water upwards through the plant. Water is also needed to transport minerals and other essential nutrients throughout the plant. “The dual role of water loss is critical for plant survival.” Define transpiration and state one primary benefit for the plant. [Understanding] Answer: Transpiration is the loss of water vapor from the aerial parts of the plant, primarily the leaves, through the stomata. A primary benefit is the cooling of the plant's surface, preventing heat damage. Scenario: On a hot, sunny afternoon, the rate of transpiration is very high. Describe the consequence of this high rate on the movement of minerals within the plant. [Application] Answer: A high rate of transpiration increases the 'transpirational pull' force. This strong upward pull increases the speed at which the water column moves through the xylem, consequently accelerating the transport and distribution of dissolved minerals to all aerial parts of the plant. How does transpiration indirectly help the root system absorb more water from the soil? [Analysis] Answer: Transpiration causes a continuous loss of water from the leaves, which in turn generates a strong negative pressure (tension or suction force) in the xylem vessels. This tension extends down to the root xylem, effectively pulling the water column upwards and creating the main driving force for water absorption from the soil.
4. Plants require a diverse range of mineral nutrients, which they obtain from the soil through their root system. These minerals, including nitrogen for protein synthesis, phosphorus for energy transfer, and potassium for water regulation, are absorbed as dissolved salts and ions. “Mineral nutrients are essential for maintaining structural integrity and driving metabolic processes.” Mention two specific functions of mineral nutrients in plant life. [Understanding] Answer: Two specific functions are: 1. Strengthening cell walls (e.g., Calcium). 2. Enabling enzyme production and powering essential metabolic processes (e.g., Magnesium for chlorophyll). Scenario: A farmer notices that the leaves of his corn plants are yellowing, starting with the older leaves. He suspects a mineral deficiency. Which mineral is most likely deficient, and why? [Application] Answer: Nitrogen is the most likely deficient mineral. Nitrogen is highly mobile in the plant and is a component of chlorophyll. When deficient, the plant mobilizes nitrogen from older leaves to supply the new growth, causing the older leaves to turn yellow (chlorosis). Why must mineral nutrients be absorbed as dissolved salts and ions rather than in their solid form? [Analysis] Answer: Minerals must be absorbed as dissolved ions because the plant's cell membranes (which control entry) are selectively permeable and cannot take up large, solid particles. Dissolved ions can move across the cell membranes via mechanisms like diffusion or, more commonly, active transport, allowing for controlled and efficient uptake.
5. Roots have evolved an impressive ability to draw water from the soil. This ability is a result of a combination of factors: a huge surface area provided by rootlets and root hairs, a higher concentration of cell sap within root hairs compared to the surrounding soil water, and thin walls of root hairs. “The morphology and internal composition of roots are optimized for efficient water uptake.” List three key characteristics of roots that make them exceptionally adept at absorbing water. [Understanding] Answer: The three key characteristics are: 1. Massive surface area due to numerous rootlets and root hairs. 2. Higher concentration of cell sap in the root hairs. 3. Thin, permeable cell walls and a semi-permeable cell membrane in the root hairs. Scenario: A plant is grown in soil that is heavily saturated with saline (salty) water. Predict the immediate consequence on the root hair cells, and state the process involved. [Application] Answer: The root hair cells will lose water and shrink (plasmolyze/flaccid) because the saline soil water has a lower water potential (higher solute concentration) than the cell sap. The process involved is Exosmosis. How does the higher solute concentration of the root hair cell sap facilitate water absorption? [Analysis] Answer: The higher concentration of solutes (salts, sugars) in the cell sap compared to the surrounding soil water creates a water potential gradient. This gradient dictates that water molecules will move spontaneously from the region of higher water potential (dilute soil water) to the region of lower water potential (concentrated cell sap) via osmosis.
6. Root hairs are specialized extensions of epidermal cells that play a pivotal role in water absorption. They contain cell sap with a higher concentration of solutes than the surrounding soil water. This concentration gradient drives water movement, as water naturally travels from a region of higher concentration (dilute solution) to a region of lower concentration (concentrated solution) through a semi-permeable membrane, a process known as osmosis. “The root hair structure and composition are the primary sites for water entry.” What is the function of the root hair in the process of absorption? [Understanding] Answer: The root hair significantly increases the surface area for absorption of water and mineral ions from the soil. It is the site where water initially enters the plant from the soil via osmosis. Scenario: A root hair cell's cell membrane becomes damaged and is no longer selectively permeable. What effect would this have on the absorption of water and solutes? [Application] Answer: The semi-permeable nature is lost. Water absorption by osmosis would cease to be efficient, and there would be an unregulated, non-selective movement of solutes and water molecules across the membrane, disrupting the necessary concentration gradient and potentially killing the cell. Explain why the thinness of the root hair cell wall is advantageous for the process of absorption. [Analysis] Answer: The thin, fully permeable cellulose cell wall offers minimal resistance to the movement of water and dissolved substances. It allows for the free movement of both water molecules and solutes up to the selectively permeable cell membrane, facilitating rapid and efficient uptake.
7. Imbibition is a fascinating process in which living or dead plant cells absorb water through surface attraction, causing them to swell. It's driven by the strong attraction between the water molecules and the cell's internal structures. This process is essential for the seed to break dormancy and begin to grow. “Water uptake can be passive and driven by physical forces, not just gradients.” Define Imbibition and state one crucial life function in plants where it is involved. [Understanding] Answer: Imbibition is the passive absorption of water by solid substances (colloids) in a living or dead cell through surface attraction, causing them to swell. It is crucial for seed germination. Scenario: Dry wooden doors swell and jam during the rainy season. Relate this phenomenon to a plant process studied in root absorption. [Application] Answer: This phenomenon is an example of Imbibition. The colloidal substances (cellulose and proteins) in the wood absorb water from the humid air, causing the wood to swell, similar to how the colloids in a dry seed absorb water to break dormancy. Explain how the imbibitional pressure generated by a germinating seed is vital for its growth. [Analysis] Answer: The swelling due to imbibition generates a massive force known as imbibitional pressure. This pressure is powerful enough to rupture the hard seed coat, allowing the radicle (embryonic root) to emerge and begin the growth of the seedling.
8. Diffusion is a fundamental process in which molecules move from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration when in direct contact. This movement occurs because molecules are constantly in motion, and they tend to spread out until they are evenly distributed (equilibrium). “The random motion of molecules drives this key passive transport mechanism.” State the principle that governs the direction of molecular movement during diffusion. [Understanding] Answer: Diffusion is governed by the principle that molecules move down their concentration gradient, meaning they travel from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration. Scenario: A plant cell is placed in a solution containing a high concentration of a specific non-polar mineral ion that the cell needs. How would the cell initially take up this ion? [Application] Answer: The ion would initially move into the cell via Simple Diffusion, because its concentration is higher outside the cell, and non-polar molecules can pass through the lipid bilayer of the cell membrane, moving down the concentration gradient until equilibrium is reached. In the context of gas exchange, why is diffusion sufficient for the movement of CO2\text{CO}_2 into the leaf cells? [Analysis] Answer: Diffusion is sufficient because the constant utilization of CO2\text{CO}_2 during photosynthesis maintains a very low concentration of CO2\text{CO}_2 inside the leaf cells (sub-stomatal cavity) compared to the atmosphere. This continuous and steep concentration gradient ensures a rapid and steady inward flow of CO2\text{CO}_2.
9. Osmosis is a specialized type of diffusion that involves the movement of water molecules from a region of higher concentration (dilute solution) to a region of lower concentration (concentrated solution) through a semi-permeable membrane. The semi-permeable membrane allows some molecules to pass through, while others are blocked, creating a selective flow of water. “Osmosis is the key process for water movement across biological membranes.” State the precise condition under which osmosis occurs. [Understanding] Answer: Osmosis occurs when two solutions of different solute concentrations are separated by a semi-permeable membrane, resulting in the net movement of water molecules from the region of higher water potential (dilute solution) to the region of lower water potential (concentrated solution). Scenario: An experiment is set up where a peeled potato cup filled with a concentrated sugar solution is placed in a beaker of water. State and explain the observation after a few hours. [Application] Answer: The sugar solution level inside the potato cup will rise. This happens because water molecules move from the beaker (higher water potential/dilute solution) across the potato tissues (acting as a semi-permeable membrane) into the sugar solution (lower water potential/concentrated solution) by osmosis. Why is the cell membrane in a plant cell described as 'selectively permeable' rather than 'semi-permeable' in modern biology? [Analysis] Answer: The cell membrane is called 'selectively permeable' because it does more than just allow small molecules (like water) to pass while blocking large ones (like a semi-permeable membrane). It actively selects and regulates the passage of specific ions and molecules, often using protein channels or carriers, based on the cell's immediate need.
10. Endosmosis is the inward diffusion of water through a semi-permeable membrane. It occurs when the surrounding solution is less concentrated than the solution inside the cell, causing the cell to swell. Exosmosis is the outward diffusion of water, occurring when the surrounding solution is more concentrated, causing the cell to shrink. “The direction of water movement is determined by the concentration of the surrounding medium.” Differentiate between Endosmosis and Exosmosis based on the relative concentration of the surrounding solution. [Understanding] Answer: Endosmosis occurs when the surrounding solution is hypotonic (less concentrated/higher water potential) than the cell sap. Exosmosis occurs when the surrounding solution is hypertonic (more concentrated/lower water potential) than the cell sap. Scenario: A cell is transferred from a 0.1%0.1\% NaCl\text{NaCl} solution to a 5%5\% NaCl\text{NaCl} solution. Which process (Endosmosis/Exosmosis) will immediately begin to occur, and why? [Application] Answer: Exosmosis will immediately begin. The 5%5\% NaCl\text{NaCl} solution is a hypertonic solution (more concentrated/lower water potential). As a result, water will move out of the cell into the surrounding solution, causing the cell to shrink. How does a plant cell's rigid cell wall prevent it from bursting when undergoing prolonged endosmosis? [Analysis] Answer: As the cell absorbs water via endosmosis, the cell volume increases, and the internal contents exert pressure (turgor pressure) against the cell wall. The rigid cell wall, being strong and inelastic, exerts an equal and opposite force (wall pressure), which prevents the cell from taking in any more water and bursting.
11. Turgidity is the state of a cell when it is fully distended due to water absorption, providing mechanical support. Turgor pressure is the pressure exerted by the cell contents against the cell wall. Flaccidity occurs when a cell loses water, causing it to become limp, soft, and pliable. “The water content defines the rigidity and shape of the plant cell.” What is the state of a plant cell when turgor pressure is at its maximum? [Understanding] Answer: When turgor pressure is at its maximum, the cell is in a state of Turgidity, meaning it is fully distended due to water absorption. Scenario: A young shoot that was wilted (flaccid) is placed in a bucket of water for an hour. Explain the recovery process and the role of turgor pressure in the final result. [Application] Answer: The cells of the shoot will absorb water by endosmosis, leading to an increase in volume and the building up of turgor pressure. This pressure will distend the cell contents against the cell walls, restoring the cell's rigidity and causing the shoot to stand upright and become turgid. Explain the relationship between Turgor Pressure and Wall Pressure in a fully turgid cell. [Analysis] Answer: In a fully turgid cell, Turgor Pressure (the pressure exerted by the cell sap/protoplast on the cell wall) is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction to Wall Pressure (the pressure exerted by the cell wall back onto the protoplast). This equality maintains the cell's constant shape and prevents further net water entry.
12. Plasmolysis is the process where the cytoplasm of a plant cell contracts away from the cell wall due to the loss of water. This occurs when the cell is placed in a hypertonic solution. Deplasmolysis is the process of rehydration where the protoplasm of a plasmolyzed cell swells back to its original state, happening in a hypotonic solution. “Plasmolysis and deplasmolysis demonstrate the effects of osmosis on the plant cell protoplasm.” Define Plasmolysis and state the type of solution that causes it. [Understanding] Answer: Plasmolysis is the process where the protoplast (cytoplasm and vacuole) of a plant cell shrinks and pulls away from the cell wall due to the loss of water (exosmosis). It is caused by placing the cell in a Hypertonic solution. Scenario: A laboratory assistant places a strip of plant tissue into a concentrated sugar solution, and after 30 minutes, the cells are plasmolyzed. What simple action must the assistant take to achieve deplasmolysis? [Application] Answer: The assistant must transfer the plasmolyzed plant tissue from the concentrated sugar solution (hypertonic) and place it into pure water or a very dilute solution (hypotonic solution), allowing the cell to reabsorb water by endosmosis and the protoplast to swell back to its original position. How can the phenomenon of plasmolysis be used to determine the relative concentration of the cell sap? [Analysis] Answer: Plasmolysis can be used to determine the concentration by finding the Isotonic Point. By testing the cell in various concentrations of a known solution, the concentration at which incipient plasmolysis (just beginning to pull away) occurs is considered the osmotic concentration equivalent to the cell sap.
13. Active transport is a remarkable process that allows substances to move from a region of lower concentration to a region of higher concentration, defying the natural flow of diffusion. This uphill movement requires energy, which is supplied by the cell in the form of ATP. “Nutrient uptake often requires energy expenditure by the plant.” What is the primary characteristic that distinguishes Active Transport from Diffusion and Osmosis? [Understanding] Answer: Active transport is distinguished by its requirement for metabolic energy (ATP) because it moves substances against their concentration gradient, from a region of lower concentration to a region of higher concentration. Scenario: The concentration of nitrate ions in the soil water is 0.01%0.01\%, but the concentration inside the root hair cell is 0.1%0.1\%. How will the root hair cell absorb more nitrate ions? [Application] Answer: The root hair cell will absorb the nitrate ions via Active Transport. Since the ions need to move from a lower concentration (soil) to a higher concentration (cell), metabolic energy (ATP) is required to power specific carrier proteins in the cell membrane to pump the ions inwards. Why is active transport absolutely necessary for the efficient uptake of minerals by roots, despite diffusion also being an available mechanism? [Analysis] Answer: Active transport is necessary because the concentration of essential mineral ions in the soil is often much lower than the concentration required inside the root cells. To accumulate these ions against a steep concentration gradient (uphill movement), the cell must expend energy to force their uptake.
14. Root pressure is the pressure that develops in the roots due to the continuous inward movement of water. This pressure provides the initial force for the upward movement of sap through the xylem. This pressure is driven by the difference in water potential between the root cells and the surrounding soil water. “An internal, positive pressure helps drive water upwards in the plant stem.” Define Root Pressure and state the mechanism that is responsible for its generation. [Understanding] Answer: Root pressure is the positive hydrostatic pressure that develops in the root xylem sap due to the continuous, active uptake of mineral ions by the root cells, which is followed by the osmotic entry of water into the xylem. Scenario: A scientist cuts the stem of a well-watered potted plant near the base and observes drops of liquid oozing out of the cut end. What is the name of this liquid, and what force causes it to be exuded? [Application] Answer: The liquid is Xylem Sap (mostly water and dissolved minerals). The force causing it to be exuded (pushed out) is Root Pressure, which is generated by the osmotic entry of water into the root xylem vessels. Why is root pressure generally an insufficient force to account for the total ascent of sap in tall trees? [Analysis] Answer: Root pressure is a relatively weak force, typically capable of lifting water only a few meters. In tall trees, the strong force of Transpirational Pull, which creates a huge suction tension, is the primary mechanism required to overcome gravity and push the water column hundreds of feet high.
15. Guttation is a process where water is expelled from the tips or margins of leaves due to root pressure. This phenomenon is often observed in certain plants, especially during the night when transpiration is low. The water droplets are typically seen at the leaf margins, where the pressure from the xylem is high enough to force water out through specialized structures. “A visible sign of root pressure activity at the leaf margins.” State the conditions under which Guttation is most likely to be observed. [Understanding] Answer: Guttation is most likely observed at night or in the early morning, when the atmosphere is humid (low transpiration rate) and the soil is moist (allowing for high root pressure). Scenario: A student observes small drops of water on the tips of grass blades early in the morning, which they initially mistake for dew. How can they confirm that the phenomenon is actually guttation? [Application] Answer: The student can confirm it is guttation by observing the location of the drops. Guttation water is exuded from the tips or margins of the leaves through specialized pores (hydathodes), whereas dew forms randomly across the entire leaf surface as condensation from the atmosphere. How does the low rate of transpiration at night contribute to the occurrence of guttation? [Analysis] Answer: During the night, the stomata are closed, causing transpiration to nearly cease. However, the roots continue to actively absorb minerals and water, generating root pressure. With no transpirational pull to relieve this pressure, the accumulating water is forced out of the hydathodes, resulting in guttation.
16. The upward movement of sap from the roots to the leaves is a complex process driven by several forces working together. These forces include root pressure, capillarity, transpirational pull, and adhesion, which helps water molecules stick to the walls of xylem vessels. “The Ascent of Sap is a coordinated effort of physical and biological forces.” List the three primary forces collectively responsible for the Ascent of Sap in tall plants. [Understanding] Answer: The three primary forces are: 1. Transpirational Pull (Suction Force) 2. Cohesion and Adhesion (Tension-Cohesion Theory) 3. Capillarity Scenario: A plant is grown in a dark, humid environment for a week. Which of the four forces driving the ascent of sap would be severely limited, and what is the consequence? [Application] Answer: Transpirational Pull would be severely limited because the dark and humid conditions greatly reduce the rate of transpiration. The consequence is that the primary force for lifting water in tall plants is absent, causing the water movement to slow drastically, relying only on root pressure and capillarity. Explain the role of Capillarity in the movement of water through the xylem vessels. [Analysis] Answer: Capillarity is the tendency of a liquid to rise in a narrow tube. Since the xylem vessels are extremely narrow (capillary), the forces of adhesion (water molecules sticking to the xylem walls) and cohesion (water molecules sticking to each other) collectively cause the water column to rise slightly, providing a continuous surface for the water column.
17. Cohesion is the attraction between water molecules, which helps maintain a continuous column of water in the xylem. Adhesion is the attraction between water molecules and the surfaces of plant cells, which helps draw more water molecules from below. These combined forces ensure that the continuous flow of water through the xylem remains unbroken. “Water molecules exhibit strong internal and external attractive forces crucial for transport.” Differentiate between Cohesion and Adhesion in the context of water transport in plants. [Understanding] Answer: Cohesion is the force of attraction between like molecules, i.e., water molecules, which keeps the water column intact. Adhesion is the force of attraction between unlike molecules, i.e., water molecules and the inner walls of the xylem vessels. Scenario: A microscopic bubble of air (embolism) enters the xylem vessel. Explain how the Cohesion-Tension theory is immediately compromised. [Application] Answer: The air bubble breaks the continuous column of water. Since cohesion is the force holding the water molecules together, the column breaks, and the transpirational pull can no longer exert tension across the gap, thereby disrupting the upward flow of sap in that specific vessel. How does the lignified wall structure of the xylem vessel complement the forces of cohesion and adhesion? [Analysis] Answer: Lignification makes the xylem walls rigid and strong. This rigidity is essential because the transpirational pull generates tremendous tension (negative pressure) in the water column. The strong, lignified walls prevent the xylem vessels from collapsing inwards due to this powerful suction force.
18. Experiment 1: To Show that Roots Absorb Water. A healthy plant with intact roots is placed in a test tube filled with water, and the initial water level is marked. Over several hours, the water level gradually decreases, indicating active water absorption by the roots. “A simple experiment to prove the fundamental function of roots.” What is the observable final result that conclusively proves that the roots absorbed water? [Understanding] Answer: The observable final result is the difference between the initial and final water levels; the significant decrease in the water level in the test tube clearly demonstrates that the roots have absorbed water. Scenario: A control setup is included in the experiment, which is an empty test tube (no plant) next to the experimental setup. After 24 hours, the water level decreases in both setups. How must the final result be calculated to isolate the water absorbed by the plant? [Application] Answer: To isolate the water absorbed by the plant, the volume of water lost due to evaporation (measured by the control setup's water loss) must be subtracted from the total volume of water lost in the experimental setup (containing the plant). Why is it essential to use a healthy plant with intact roots for this experiment? [Analysis] Answer: It is essential because water absorption is an active physiological process involving living root hairs and the mechanisms of osmosis and active transport. Damaged or unhealthy roots would not function properly, leading to inaccurate or negligible water absorption.
19. Experiment 2: To Show that Water is Conducted Upwards through the Xylem. A young balsam plant is placed in water containing red dye (eosin solution). Within minutes, the red coloring begins to appear in the stem, and eventually, the leaf veins. When the stem is cut horizontally, red-stained xylem vessels are clearly visible. “Visual evidence of the pathway for water conduction.” Name the specific tissue that is stained red in the final observation of this experiment. [Understanding] Answer: The Xylem vessels (or xylem tissue) are the specific tissues stained red in the stem and leaf veins. Scenario: A scientist performs this experiment on two plants: one in a cool, humid room and another in a hot, dry room. In which plant will the red dye reach the leaves faster, and why? [Application] Answer: The dye will reach the leaves faster in the plant in the hot, dry room. The hot, dry conditions increase the rate of transpiration, which in turn increases the transpirational pull, accelerating the upward movement of the water/dye column through the xylem. How does this experiment conclusively prove that water transport is primarily the function of the xylem and not the phloem? [Analysis] Answer: The experiment proves this because only the xylem vessels are stained with the dye, while the phloem remains unstained. Since the dye moves along the pathway of water transport and only the xylem is colored, it conclusively identifies the xylem as the specific tissue responsible for water conduction.
20. Experiment 3: Conduction of Water through Xylem. This experiment compares two shoots: one with the phloem removed (ring of bark removed, xylem intact) and one with the xylem removed (a central plug removed). The shoot with phloem removed remains healthy and turgid, while the shoot with xylem removed quickly wilts and droops. “A controlled comparison to isolate the function of vascular tissues in water transport.” Based on the results of this experiment, state the primary function of the xylem. [Understanding] Answer: The primary function of the xylem is the upward conduction of water and dissolved minerals from the roots to the aerial parts of the plant. Scenario: A garden plant's stem is accidentally damaged such that only the phloem tissue is scraped away, but the xylem remains unharmed. Predict the long-term impact on the plant's roots. [Application] Answer: In the long term, the roots will starve and die. Phloem is responsible for the downward transport of food (sugars) from the leaves. Without phloem, the roots will not receive the nutrients needed for respiration, growth, and survival. Why is the wilting of the shoot with the xylem removed a more critical and immediate observation than the fate of the shoot with phloem removed? [Analysis] Answer: Water transport (xylem) is crucial for maintaining turgidity and preventing immediate dehydration, so its interruption leads to rapid wilting and death. Food transport (phloem) is a slower process, and the shoot has sufficient stored food to remain functional and turgid for a longer period, making wilting a more immediate indicator of xylem failure.
21. Experiment 4: To Show that Food from the Leaves is Conducted Downwards through the Phloem (Girdling Experiment). By removing a ring of bark, which contains the phloem, the area above the girdle swells due to the accumulation of food materials that cannot move downward, while the portion below the girdle progressively thins. “Demonstrating the role of phloem in translocation.” Identify the specific tissue removed when a ring of bark is cut away, and state its function. [Understanding] Answer: The Phloem tissue is removed (as it is located in the inner bark). Its function is the downward conduction (translocation) of prepared food (sugars) from the leaves to the rest of the plant, including the roots. Scenario: After performing the girdling experiment on a tree, an insect begins feeding on the swollen ring above the cut. Why is this upper area a particularly rich source of nutrients for the insect? [Application] Answer: The area above the cut is a rich source of nutrients because the downward flow of sugars (photosynthesis products) from the leaves has been blocked by the removal of the phloem. These accumulated sugars cause the swelling, making the tissue above the girdle nutrient-dense. How does the long-term impact on the roots (starvation) conclusively prove that phloem is essential for food conduction? [Analysis] Answer: Since the roots are not photosynthetic, they depend entirely on the food translocated from the leaves via the phloem for energy and growth. The fact that the roots starve and thin only when the phloem pathway is interrupted (girdled) confirms that the phloem is the only viable route for the downward transport of food materials.
22. Water is vital for plant cells, playing a crucial role in their functioning as the medium of the protoplasm. Water is also essential for transpiration, cooling the plant and drawing water upwards, and for providing turgidity, keeping plant cells plump and rigid. “Water is the matrix of life and structure in plants.” State two distinct life processes in plants that require water to act as a medium or solvent. [Understanding] Answer: Two distinct processes are: 1. Metabolism/Cell Functioning: Water is the main component of protoplasm, providing the medium for all biochemical reactions. 2. Transport: Water acts as the solvent for transporting nutrients (minerals via xylem, food via phloem) throughout the plant. Scenario: A cell is dehydrated, and its protoplasm volume decreases. Describe the immediate consequence on the efficiency of the cell's metabolic reactions. [Application] Answer: The efficiency of the metabolic reactions will decrease. Dehydration reduces the water content of the protoplasm, leading to a higher concentration of solutes, which can slow down or disrupt the optimal functioning of enzymes and the necessary substrate-product movement. Why is the continuous availability of water a prerequisite for the survival of enzymes within the cell? [Analysis] Answer: Enzymes require an aqueous (water-based) medium to maintain their specific three-dimensional structure (conformation). Water acts as a solvent and medium; without sufficient water, enzymes denature (lose their functional structure), halting all vital metabolic processes and leading to cell death.
23. Rootlets and root hairs provide a huge surface area, crucial for maximizing contact with the soil, enabling the plant to efficiently absorb water and minerals. The root system of a small garden plant, if its root hairs were laid end to end, could stretch for kilometers. “The extensive network of the root system is an adaptation for maximum absorption.” Name the specific extensions of the epidermal cells of the root that are responsible for the maximum absorption surface area. [Understanding] Answer: Root hairs (specialized extensions of the epidermal cells in the Area of Maturation). Scenario: Due to poor tillage, the soil around a plant is highly compacted. How would this compaction indirectly affect the functional surface area of the root? [Application] Answer: Compaction reduces the number and length of new root hairs that can penetrate the soil effectively. It also limits the oxygen supply, hindering root respiration and thus reducing the overall functional surface area available for efficient absorption. How does the root hair's location in the 'Area of Maturation' specifically benefit the process of absorption? [Analysis] Answer: The Area of Maturation is located behind the zones of elongation and cell division. This location is beneficial because the cells are fully differentiated and equipped with mature vacuoles and functioning cell membranes, which are necessary to establish the required high solute concentration gradient for osmosis and active transport.
24. The xylem, a vascular tissue, is responsible for the one-way flow of water and minerals upwards, consisting of non-living, hollow cells stiffened with lignin and having no end walls between cells. The phloem, another vascular tissue, is responsible for the two-way flow of food, consisting of living cells with end walls containing perforations (sieve plates). “The structural differences between xylem and phloem dictate their transport functions.” State one structural difference between the xylem vessels and phloem sieve tubes. [Understanding] Answer: Xylem vessels are non-living (dead) and stiffened with lignin, with no end walls, allowing for continuous water flow. 25. Diffusion is a fundamental process in which molecules move from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration when in direct contact. [Application] Answer: Lack of oxygen would primarily inhibit Active Transport (not diffusion), as the root cells would not be able to respire aerobically and produce the necessary ATP. Why is the rate of diffusion faster in gases than in liquids? [Analysis] Answer: The rate of diffusion is faster in gases because the molecules are much further apart and move much more rapidly and randomly compared to molecules in a liquid. 26. Osmosis is the movement of water through a semi-permeable membrane from a region of higher water potential to a region of lower water potential, driving water uptake by roots. “Water potential is the modern term for the driving force of osmosis.” Define the term 'Water Potential'. [Understanding] Answer: Water Potential (Ψ\Psi) is the measure of the potential energy of water per unit volume relative to pure water (which has a potential of zero). Water moves from an area of higher water potential to an area of lower water potential. In which direction will the net movement of water occur? [Application] Answer: The net movement of water will be out of the cell, into the soil. Water moves from higher water potential (less negative, −50 kPa-50 \text{ kPa}) to lower water potential (more negative, −100 kPa-100 \text{ kPa}). Why is it more accurate to describe the movement of water during osmosis in terms of 'water potential' rather than 'solute concentration'? [Analysis] Answer: Water potential is a comprehensive term that accounts for both solute concentration (solute potential) and pressure exerted on the water (pressure potential, like turgor pressure). It provides a more precise and accurate measure of the free energy available for water movement, which is the true driving force, unlike concentration, which only considers one component.
27. They possess a binding site that can only recognize and bind to a specific ion or molecule (like a lock-and-key mechanism). The protein then undergoes a conformational change, using ATP energy to translocate only that specific substance across the membrane, ensuring selectivity.

33. Plasmolysis is the process where the cytoplasm of a plant cell contracts away from the cell wall due to the loss of water. 34. Deplasmolysis is the process of rehydration where the protoplasm of a plasmolyzed cell swells back to its original state. The Importance of Water and Minerals is comprehensive, covering structural roles (turgidity), metabolic roles (photosynthesis, enzyme action), and transport roles. “Water is the universal requirement for all plant functions.” Name the phenomenon in which water helps to cool the plant. [Understanding] Answer: Transpiration (the evaporation of water from the leaf surface) is the phenomenon that helps to cool the plant. Scenario: During a hot day, a plant's leaves start to overheat. What change in the stomatal activity is expected to occur in response, and why? [Application] Answer: The stomata are expected to open wider (if water is available). This increases the rate of transpiration, which facilitates evaporative cooling to lower the leaf temperature and prevent heat damage. How does the plant balance the conflicting needs of CO2\text{CO}_2 uptake (stomata open) and water conservation (stomata closed)? [Analysis] Answer: The plant uses the Guard Cells as a regulatory mechanism. When water is plentiful, the guard cells become turgid and open the stomata for CO2\text{CO}_2 uptake. When water is scarce, the guard cells become flaccid and close the stomata, prioritizing water conservation, even at the cost of reduced photosynthesis.
